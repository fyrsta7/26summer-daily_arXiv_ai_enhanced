### [1] [RaReCache: Bridging the Gap in Cross-Model KV Cache Reuse via Rank disagreement-based Selective Recomputation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11358)
*Sreetama Sarkar,Saptarshi Mitra,Sitao Huang,Souvik Kundu,Peter A. Beerel*

Main category: cs.AI



Selection reason: 面向 coding agents 与多模型系统的跨模型 KV cache 复用和选择性重算，可迁移到 agent 服务效率与多模型级联基础设施

TL;DR: RaReCache 通过识别少量信息密集 token 并选择性重计算，实现跨模型 KV 缓存复用；在 23 倍参数差下重算 30% 保留 95-99% 准确率，最高 3.04 倍预填充加速，在线吞吐达目标预填充 1.8 倍，并大幅降低 TTFT。


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: 跨模型 KV 缓存复用是现代 LLM 服务的关键挑战，模型切换或级联升级通常需从头预填充；同族闭式线性映射随模型规模差距增大而迁移准确率下降，且失败集中在少量信息密集 token。

Method: 提出 RaReCache：利用新颖的秩不一致性度量，按映射 KV 在校准数据弱支持输出方向上的能量对 token 打分，识别关键位置并进行选择性重计算，使大目标模型能从较小源模型预填充的缓存中准确解码。

Result: 在两个模型族和五个基准上，Qwen3-0.6B 到 14B（23 倍参数差）重算 30% 位置保留 95-99% 目标准确率；Llama3-8B 到 70B（8.8 倍差）重算 40% 保留 96.5%；最高 3.04 倍预填充加速；单 GPU 在线吞吐为目标预填充 1.8 倍，饱和负载下 30% 重算预算使中位数与 99 分位 TTFT 分别降低 5.0 倍和 6.4 倍。

Conclusion: RaReCache 建立了小模型代大规模目标模型预填充、大模型仅重计算关键 token 的高效服务范式，大幅降低预填充延迟，并基本消除对源模型规模的敏感性。

中文摘要：跨模型 KV 缓存复用仍然是现代 LLM 服务中的关键挑战。编码代理和多模型系统越来越多地在不同模型之间路由共享上下文：用户可能在会话中途切换模型，或者级联系统可能升级处理困难查询。由于 KV 缓存包含模型特定的表示，每次切换通常都迫使接收模型从头预填充整个上下文。近期工作表明，闭式线性映射可以在同一族模型之间转换 KV 缓存，但随着模型规模差距扩大，迁移准确率会下降。本文中，我们确立这些迁移失败集中在少量信息密集的 token 上。为弥合这一差距，我们提出 RaReCache，一个框架，使大型目标模型能够通过选择性重计算，从由小得多的源模型预填充的缓存中准确解码。RaReCache 使用一种新颖的秩不一致性（rank disagreement）度量来识别这些关键位置，通过将每个 token 的映射 KV 在校准数据支持较弱的输出方向上的能量进行打分。在两个模型族和五个基准上，在 23 倍参数差距（Qwen3-0.6B 到 14B）下，仅重计算 30% 的位置即可保留目标准确率的 95-99%；而在 8.8 倍差距（Llama3-8B 到 70B）下，重计算 40% 可保留目标准确率的 96.5%。RaReCache 在很大程度上消除了对源模型规模的敏感性，并实现了最高 3.04 倍的预填充加速。对于在线服务，在单块 GPU 上，其请求吞吐量达到目标预填充的 1.8 倍；在目标模型的饱和负载下，以 30% 重计算预算，将中位数和 99 分位首 token 时间（TTFT）分别降低 5.0 倍和 6.4 倍。RaReCache 建立了一种高效的服务范式：小模型代表大规模目标模型进行预填充，使大模型仅重计算关键 token，从而大幅降低预填充延迟。

Original abstract: Cross-model KV-cache reuse remains a key challenge in modern LLM serving. Coding agents and multi-model systems increasingly route a shared context across models: a user may switch models mid-session, or a cascade may escalate a difficult query. Because KV caches contain model-specific representations, each switch typically forces the receiving model to prefill the entire context from scratch. Recent work shows that closed-form linear maps can translate KV caches between models in the same family, but transfer accuracy degrades as the model-size gap widens. In this paper, we establish that these transfer failures are concentrated in a small subset of information-dense tokens. To bridge this gap, we introduce RaReCache, a framework that enables a large target model to decode accurately from a cache prefilled by a much smaller source via selective recomputation. RaReCache identifies these critical positions using a novel rank disagreement metric, scoring each token by the energy of its mapped KV in output directions weakly supported by the calibration data. Across two model families and five benchmarks, on a 23x parameter gap (Qwen3-0.6B to 14B) recomputing just 30% of positions retains 95-99% of the target accuracy, whereas on a 8.8x gap (Llama3-8B to 70B), recomputing 40% retains 96.5% of the target accuracy. RaReCache largely removes sensitivity to source-model size, and achieves up to a 3.04x prefill speedup. For online serving, it handles 1.8x the request throughput of target prefill on a single GPU, and at the target's saturation load, reduces median and 99th-percentile time-to-first-token (TTFT) by 5.0x and 6.4x respectively, with a 30% recompute budget. RaReCache establishes an efficient serving paradigm where small models prefill on behalf of massive targets, enabling large models to recompute only critical tokens, drastically reducing prefill latency.

Original abstract: Large language models increasingly justify legal decisions by naming the statute or precedent behind a verdict, treated as evidence that the decision follows from it. We test this directly: holding case facts fixed, we substitute the named legal authority for an unrelated one and decode a model's evolving verdict from its hidden states. Across seven open-weight models (8B-70B) and four benchmarks spanning judicial and contractual reasoning, when explicitly required to justify a verdict by naming the governing authority, models name the correct one in 66.7%-100% of generations, while the verdict changing when the authority changes is far less consistent: 0.0%-21.7% on CaseHOLD, 30.0%-76.7% on ECHR and SCOTUS, and 43.3%-50.0% on ContractNLI. Neither scale nor a purpose-built legal-reasoning model (a best-effort LoRA reproduction; Section 6) closes this gap. A red-teaming evaluation on five core models finds compliance with an adversarial instruction hidden in the case facts (73.3%-96.4%) exceeds verdict-swap sensitivity by a wide margin, holding without exception across model rankings. Naming a legal authority is thus a poor proxy for a verdict's dependence on it, while the same verdict remains separately vulnerable to adversarial manipulation. Both findings replicate across checks ruling out prompt-wording noise and confounded sampling, and bear directly on the use of generated legal explanations as compliance or audit artefacts.
